The honest answer first

Learners often ask me which Malayalam certificate to take. The truth is that the choice is small. Unlike French (DELF) or Japanese (JLPT), Malayalam has no widely recognised international proficiency exam for adult learners. SpeakMalayalam's one-to-one courses come with a certificate of completion signed by me, which records the level you finished but is not an official qualification. What does exist is a government programme for children of the Malayali diaspora, school and university qualifications, and informal level checks. Here is what each one is, who it suits, and how to prepare.

Your options at a glance

OptionWho it suitsWhat you get
Malayalam Mission (Government of Kerala)Children and teenagers of Malayali families living outside KeralaA staged course taught at local learning centres; completing the final stage leads to a certificate the Mission describes as equivalent to Class 10 Malayalam
School boards in IndiaSchool students in India, including CBSE and Kerala state board schoolsMalayalam as an examined language subject on the school certificate
University courses in KeralaAdults who want an academic qualificationCertificate, diploma and degree programmes in Malayalam language and literature
Informal level checkAdult learners who want to know where they standA placement result, not a credential: try the Malayalam level test

Malayalam Mission in more detail

Malayalam Mission is a Kerala government programme that teaches Malayalam to children of the diaspora through learning centres run with local Malayali associations in India and abroad, usually by trained volunteer teachers. The course has four stages, each named after a Kerala flower:

StageNameTypical length
1കണിക്കൊന്ന (Kanikkonna)about two years
2സൂര്യകാന്തി (Sooryakanthi)about two years
3ആമ്പൽ (Aambal)about three years
4നീലക്കുറിഞ്ഞി (Neelakurinji)about three years

Timings and fees vary by chapter, so check with the Malayali association nearest you before enrolling.

What Malayalam certificates actually test

Reading and writing the script

All formal routes test the Malayalam script, including vowel signs and conjunct letters. Start with the alphabet guide and its printable chart.

Comprehension

Reading short passages and answering questions, usually in standard written Malayalam rather than spoken forms.

Grammar and vocabulary

Case endings, verb tenses and formal vocabulary. The guide to how Malayalam sentences work covers the core.

Speaking

Some programmes include recitation or oral tasks. Spoken practice also makes the written language far easier to remember.

How to prepare

  1. Months 1 to 2: learn to read the script fluently, a few letters a day.
  2. Months 2 to 4: read short, simple texts aloud daily; children's stories are ideal.
  3. Alongside: keep speaking every day so vocabulary sticks: the 50 conversation prompts are a good routine.
  4. Before the exam: practise writing answers by hand and have them checked.
