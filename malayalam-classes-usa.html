This is the first question almost every learner in the States asks, so here is the plain answer. I teach from 5:00 AM to 11:30 PM IST, and India is 9.5 hours ahead of Eastern Time (10.5 hours when the US is on standard time). In practice that gives you two comfortable windows:

Why Malayalam fades in American-born children (and what actually reverses it)

If you were born in Kerala and your children were born in the US, you have probably watched the same pattern: you speak Malayalam to them, they answer in English. They understand you perfectly, but the words will not come out.

This is called receptive bilingualism, and it is completely normal. It is not laziness and it is not too late. What happens is that a child gets enough Malayalam input to understand, but almost no situations where they are required to produce it. English does all the work at school, with friends, on screens. Comprehension grows; production stalls.

The fix is not more vocabulary lists. It is structured speaking practice with someone who will wait for the child to answer in Malayalam, gently, without shaming them — which is exactly what a parent, who is emotionally invested, finds almost impossible to do. That is the real value of an outside teacher.

Children as young as five can go from silent to conversational in a few months, because the Malayalam is already in there. We are not building from zero. We are switching it on.

Common questions from American learners

Is it realistic to learn Malayalam living in the US, with no one to practise with?

Yes, and it is the most common situation I teach. The lesson itself is your speaking practice, and between lessons you get short daily tasks — five minutes, not an hour. Many learners also practise on family video calls to Kerala, which is usually the whole reason they started.

My child understands Malayalam but refuses to speak it. Can you help?

This is the single most common request I get from Malayali parents in the US. Yes. The Malayalam is already there — the barrier is confidence and lack of situations that demand output. Lessons are built around getting the child speaking without embarrassment, and progress is usually faster than parents expect.

How long before I can hold a conversation with my in-laws?

With one lesson a week plus a few minutes of daily practice, most adult beginners manage simple, real family conversation — greetings, food, health, how the day went — within about three to four months. I will not promise fluency in thirty days, because nobody can honestly deliver that.

Do I have to learn the Malayalam script?

No. You can go a long way in spoken Malayalam using Romanised Malayalam. If you later want to read signboards in Kerala or message relatives in script, we add it — but it is optional, and it is never the starting point.

Do you teach Malayalam for travel to Kerala?

Yes. If you have a trip coming up, we can run a short, focused set of lessons on airport, taxi, restaurant, shopping and family-visit Malayalam.
