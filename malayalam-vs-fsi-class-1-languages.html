Malayalam and the Class 1 languages

Languages closely related to English — and how much of Malayalam's grammar each one already gives you.

Class 1 — 24–30 weeks · 600–750 hours. Languages closely related to English, according to the US Foreign Service Institute's published difficulty categories. The easiest languages for an English speaker are the least like Malayalam. Not one of them puts the verb last, and only Italian shares as many as three and a half of the ten features.

Shared grammatical features with Malayalam

Each language scored against the same ten features used throughout this site. A full match counts 1, a partial match 0.5.

Italian 3.5 / 10

The best Class 1 match: pro-drop, the respect ladder, and genuinely phonemic twin consonants — fato against fatto.

Romanian 3 / 10

Keeps a reduced case system, which no other Romance language does.

Spanish 2.5 / 10

Pro-drop and the tú/usted ladder — but nothing structural beyond that.

Portuguese 2.5 / 10

Pro-drop and a respect ladder; no structural overlap.

Dutch 2 / 10

Verb-final in subordinate clauses — a genuine glimpse of Malayalam word order inside a Germanic language.

French 1.5 / 10

The tu/vous choice, and impersonal datives like il me semble. Otherwise as far from Malayalam as English.

Swedish 1.5 / 10

Phonemic consonant length, and retroflex consonants in rt/rd clusters — the surprise overlap.

Norwegian 1.5 / 10

Same as Swedish: consonant length and retroflexion in clusters.

Danish 1 / 10

The lowest overlap of any language on this site apart from English itself.

Afrikaans 0.5 / 10

Verb-final in subordinate clauses; otherwise nothing.
