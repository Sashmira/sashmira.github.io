The 20 Rule Cards

These are the rules behind the rules — the twenty cards that the whole 3,297-page course is built on. If you are stuck on something in Malayalam, one of these almost always names what you are stuck on.

How to use these

Read one card. Say its example aloud three times. Move on. They are deliberately short — each one is a single idea you can hold in your head while speaking, not a chapter to study.

Card 01 · SOV

The verb goes last, every time

English puts the verb second: I eat rice. Malayalam puts it at the end: ഞാൻ ചോറ് കഴിക്കുന്നു Njaan choru kazhikkunnu — I rice eat. Card 02 · TENSE

The ending carries the time and nothing else

പോകുന്നു pokunnu (going), പോയി poyi (went), പോകും pokum (will go). The ending changes for time and for nothing else — not for I, you, he, she or they. This is the single largest saving Malayalam offers an English speaker: learn three endings and you have the whole verb for every person.

Card 03 · AANU

aanu is identity, undu is existence

ആണ് aanu says what something IS — it links a thing to a label. ഉണ്ട് undu says something EXISTS or that you HAVE it. ഞാൻ ടീച്ചർ ആണ് Njaan teacher aanu is identity; എനിക്ക് സമയം ഉണ്ട് Enikku samayam undu is possession. Card 04 · NEG

alla denies a label, illa denies a presence

അല്ല alla is the no that answers 'is it X?' — it rejects the label. ഇല്ല illa is the no that answers 'is there any?' — it rejects existence, presence or possession. ഇത് ചായ അല്ല Ithu chaaya alla means this is not tea. ചായ ഇല്ല Chaaya illa means there is no tea. Same English word, two different Malayalam thoughts.

Card 05 · CASE

Endings glue on; there are no prepositions

Where English puts a small word in front — in the house, to the shop — Malayalam glues an ending onto the back: വീട്ടിൽ veettil, കടയിലേക്ക് kadayilekku. -ിൽ -il is at or in, -ിലേക്ക് -ilekku is motion towards, -ിൽ നിന്ന് -il ninnu is from, -ോട് -odu is with or to a person. Card 07 · QW

The question words

ആര് aaru who · എവിടെ evide where · എപ്പോ eppo when · എന്താ entha what · എത്ര ethra how much · എങ്ങനെ engane how · എന്തുകൊണ്ട് enthukondu why. They sit where the missing information would go, and the rest of the sentence keeps its normal shape. You never invert the sentence the way English does.

Card 08 · RESP

nee, ningal, thaangal — the respect ladder

നീ nee is for close friends and children and can insult anyone else. നിങ്ങൾ ningal is the safe default. താങ്കൾ thaangal is ceremonial and rare in speech. In real life Malayalis dodge the whole problem with a relationship word — ചേട്ടാ chetta for an older man, ചേച്ചി chechi for an older woman — even to strangers.

Card 09 · CHAIN

Actions chain onto one final verb

Malayalam does not say and then. It hangs each action off the one verb at the end. -ഇട്ട് -ittu means having done it: കഴിച്ചിട്ട് പോയി kazhichittu poyi — ate, then left. -ആതെ -aathe means without doing it: പറയാതെ പോയി parayaathe poyi. -അപ്പോൾ -appol and -ഉമ്പോൾ -umbol both mean when — വന്നപ്പോൾ

Card 10 · ENNU

ennu is the one word for reported speech

English can drop that — I thought she would come. Malayalam never drops എന്ന് ennu. Card 11 · NOML

-athu turns a verb into a thing

Stick -അത് -athu on a verb and it becomes a noun you can talk about. പോയി poyi (went) becomes പോയത് poyathu — the going, or the fact that you went. This is the engine behind clipped Malayalam questions and answers: എന്താ കഴിച്ചത്? enthaa kazhichathu? — what was eaten? — answered

Card 12 · PROHIB

The four strengths of don't

Four ways to say don't, and they are a ladder. -അണ്ട - anda is friendly discouragement: പോവണ്ട povanda, don't bother going. -അരുത് -aruthu is a genuine instruction: പോകരുത് pokaruthu. പാടില്ല paadilla is a flat ban with authority behind it — ഇവിടെ പുകവലിക്കാൻ പാടില്ല ivide pukavalikkaan paadilla.

Card 13 · INDEF

oru, -engkilum, -okke: a, any, some

Malayalam has no a and no the, so the work moves to suffixes. ഒരു oru in front is roughly a or one: ഒരു ചായ oru chaaya. -ഏതെങ്കിലും -engkilum makes it any at all — എന്തെങ്കിലും enthengkilum anything, ആരെങ്കിലും aarengkilum anyone. -ഒക്കെ -okke sweeps a vague bunch together: എന്തൊക്കെ

The two l sounds and the two n sounds

Card 17 · CHIL

Chillu letters are consonants with no vowel

ൻ ൽ ർ ൺ ൾ are the chillu letters — a consonant standing alone at the end of a word with no vowel after it. അവൻ avan ends in ൻ n. They exist because Malayalam needs a way to write a bare final consonant, and recognising them stops you from adding a vowel that is not there.

Card 18 · SCRIPT

Reading the script: sign, not letter
