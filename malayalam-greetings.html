Malayalam Greetings & Introductions: The Complete Beginner’s Guide

By Reshmi R Nair, Malayalam Linguist · Updated October 2026

Greetings and introductions are the first five minutes of every conversation in Kerala. This guide puts them all in one place: how to say hello, good night and goodbye, how to ask someone's name, where they're from and what they do, and how to answer. Every phrase is everyday spoken Malayalam, with the script, an easy romanised spelling and the English meaning.

Hello and how are you

The standard hello is namaskaram, polite and fine at any time of day. In casual speech, though, Malayalis usually skip a formal hello and open with a question: sukhamaano? (how are you?) or enthaa vishesham? (what’s new?). A quick English “hi” is also everywhere.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
നമസ്കാരംNamaskaramHello / greetings (polite, any time)
ഹായ്HaayHi (casual, with friends)
സുഖമാണോ?Sukhamaano?How are you?
എന്താ വിശേഷം?Enthaa vishesham?What’s new? / What’s up?
സുഖം / സുഖം തന്നെSukham / Sukham thanneI’m fine / I’m well
നിങ്ങൾക്ക് സുഖമാണോ?Ningalkku sukhamaano?Are you well? (polite)

With elders or someone you’ve just met, open with namaskaram, often with palms pressed together. With friends, go straight to sukhamaano? or enthaa vishesham?

Good morning, good evening, good night

Formal time-of-day greetings exist, but in daily speech they’re rare. People use namaskaram, the English “good morning” or “good night”, or simply ask sukhamaano? At night, the warm family phrase is sukhamaayi urangoo, “sleep well.”

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
സുപ്രഭാതംSuprabhaathamGood morning (formal)
ശുഭസന്ധ്യShubhasandhyaGood evening (formal, rarely spoken)
ശുഭരാത്രിShubharaathriGood night
സുഖമായി ഉറങ്ങൂSukhamaayi urangooSleep well
നല്ല ഉറക്കംNalla urakkamSleep well (lit. good sleep)

What’s your name? / My name is…

Peru means “name.” Use ninte (your, casual) with friends, peers and children, and ningalude (your, polite) with elders and strangers.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
നിന്റെ പേര് എന്താ?Ninte peru enthaa?What’s your name? (casual)
നിങ്ങളുടെ പേര് എന്താ?Ningalude peru enthaa?What’s your name? (polite)
എന്റെ പേര് ജോൺ എന്നാണ്.Ente peru John ennaanu.My name is John.
ഞാൻ ജോൺ.Njaan John.I’m John. (casual)

Nice to meet you

Santhosham means happiness, so kandathil santhosham is literally “happy to have seen you.” Reply with enikkum santhosham, “me too.”

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
കണ്ടതിൽ സന്തോഷംKandathil santhoshamNice to meet you
പരിചയപ്പെട്ടതിൽ സന്തോഷംParichayappettathil santhoshamGlad to get to know you
എനിക്കും സന്തോഷംEnikkum santhoshamLikewise / me too

Where are you from? Where do you live?

Two different questions here. Evidunnaa? asks where someone comes from; evideyaa thaamasam? asks where they live now. Malayalis also love asking about your naadu, your hometown or native place. To say where you’re from, add -il ninnaanu (from) to the place.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
എവിടുന്നാ?Evidunnaa?Where are you from?
ഞാൻ ലണ്ടനിൽ നിന്നാണ്.Njaan Londonil ninnaanu.I’m from London.
എവിടെയാ താമസം?Evideyaa thaamasam?Where do you live?
ഞാൻ കൊച്ചിയിലാ താമസം.Njaan Kochiyilaa thaamasam.I live in Kochi.
നാട് എവിടെയാ?Naadu evideyaa?Where’s your hometown?
എന്റെ നാട് തൃശ്ശൂരാ.Ente naadu Thrissooraa.My hometown is Thrissur.

What do you do?

Joli means job or work. English job titles (teacher, engineer, doctor, nurse) are used as they are in everyday Malayalam.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
എന്താ ജോലി?Enthaa joli?What’s your job?
എന്താ ചെയ്യുന്നേ?Enthaa cheyyunne?What do you do?
ഞാൻ ഒരു ടീച്ചർ ആണ്.Njaan oru teacher aanu.I’m a teacher.
ഞാൻ പഠിക്കുവാ.Njaan padikkuvaa.I’m a student / I’m studying.

How old are you?

Vayassu means age or years. Asking a child their age is one of the warmest ways to start chatting with a Malayali family.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
നിനക്ക് എത്ര വയസ്സായി?Ninakku ethra vayassaayi?How old are you? (casual, e.g. to a child)
നിങ്ങൾക്ക് എത്ര വയസ്സായി?Ningalkku ethra vayassaayi?How old are you? (polite)
എനിക്ക് മുപ്പത് വയസ്സായി.Enikku muppathu vayassaayi.I’m thirty years old.

Goodbye and see you later

There’s no everyday word for “goodbye.” Malayalis say “see you” instead, using the pattern [time] + kaanaam: pinne kaanaam (see you later), naale kaanaam (see you tomorrow). Add tto or ketto at the end to sound friendly. A casual English “bye” is also very common.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
പിന്നെ കാണാംPinne kaanaamSee you later
വീണ്ടും കാണാംVeendum kaanaamSee you again
നാളെ കാണാംNaale kaanaamSee you tomorrow
അപ്പോ കാണാംAppo kaanaamSee you then
ശരി, പിന്നെ കാണാം ട്ടോ!Sheri, pinne kaanaam tto!Okay, see you later!

Take care

When someone heads off, especially at night or on a journey, Malayalis say sookshichu pokane, “go carefully.” It’s one of the kindest things you can say at a parting.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
സൂക്ഷിക്കണേSookshikkaneTake care
സൂക്ഷിച്ച് പോകണേSookshichu pokaneGo safely / take care on the way
ശുഭയാത്രShubhayaathraHave a good journey
പിന്നെ കാണാം, സൂക്ഷിക്കണേ!Pinne kaanaam, sookshikkane!See you later, take care!

Thank you, please, sorry and welcome

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
നന്ദിNandiThank you
വളരെ നന്ദിValare nandiThank you very much
ദയവായിDayavaayiPlease (formal; in speech, a polite tone or “-aamo?” does the job)
ക്ഷമിക്കണംKshamikkanamSorry / excuse me
സ്വാഗതംSwaagathamWelcome

Among family and close friends, Malayalis rarely say nandi; warmth is shown through tone and actions. With shopkeepers, hosts and anyone who helps you, a sincere nandi is always appreciated.

Polite and casual “you”

Malayalam has two everyday forms of “you”: nee (casual, for friends, younger people and children) and ningal (polite, for elders and strangers). They change shape in questions: ninte / ningalude (your), ninakku / ningalkku (to you). If in doubt, use the polite form. Nobody minds being treated with respect.

Put it together: a first conversation

A: Namaskaram! Sukhamaano? — Hello! How are you?

B: Sukham thanne. Ningalude peru enthaa? — I’m fine. What’s your name?

A: Ente peru Anna ennaanu. Ningalo? — My name is Anna. And you?

B: Njaan Rajan. Kandathil santhosham. Evidunnaa? — I’m Rajan. Nice to meet you. Where are you from?

A: Njaan Londonil ninnaanu. Enikkum santhosham! — I’m from London. Nice to meet you too!

B: Sheri, pinne kaanaam. Sookshichu pokane! — Okay, see you later. Take care!

Frequently asked questions

How do you say hello in Malayalam?

The polite hello is “namaskaram” (നമസ്കാരം), used at any time of day. Casually, people open with “sukhamaano?” (how are you?) or “enthaa vishesham?” (what’s new?).

How do you say goodbye in Malayalam?

There’s no everyday word for goodbye. Say “pinne kaanaam” (see you later), “veendum kaanaam” (see you again) or “naale kaanaam” (see you tomorrow).

How do you say good night in Malayalam?

“Shubharaathri” (ശുഭരാത്രി) means good night. To family, “sukhamaayi urangoo” (sleep well) sounds warmer.

How do you ask someone’s name in Malayalam?

Politely, “ningalude peru enthaa?”; casually, “ninte peru enthaa?” Answer with “ente peru ___ ennaanu” (my name is ___).

How do you say nice to meet you in Malayalam?

Say “kandathil santhosham” (കണ്ടതിൽ സന്തോഷം). The reply is “enikkum santhosham”, meaning me too.

How do you say take care in Malayalam?

Say “sookshikkane”, or when someone is leaving, “sookshichu pokane” (go safely).
