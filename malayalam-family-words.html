Malayalam Family Words: Amma, Achan, Relatives & Friends

By Reshmi R Nair, Malayalam Linguist · Updated October 2026

Every Malayali family conversation is woven from these words: amma, achan, chetta, chechi. If you grew up hearing them abroad, or you’ve married into a Malayali family, this guide makes the whole family tree clear, from parents and grandparents to in-laws, plus how to talk about friends and say you’re married. Every word comes with the Malayalam script, an easy romanised spelling and the English meaning.

Mother, father and the core family

Amma (mother) and achan (father, also spelt achchan) are the first words every Malayali child learns. Together they become achanum ammayum: father and mother.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
അമ്മAmmaMother
അച്ഛൻAchanFather
അച്ഛനും അമ്മയുംAchanum ammayumFather and mother (parents)
ചേട്ടൻChettanElder brother (address: chetta)
ചേച്ചിChechiElder sister
അനിയൻAniyanYounger brother
അനിയത്തിAniyathiYounger sister
മകൻMakanSon (affectionate: mone)
മകൾMakalDaughter (affectionate: mole)
ഭർത്താവ്BharthaavuHusband
ഭാര്യBhaaryaWife
കുടുംബംKudumbamFamily

Two of the most loving words in Malayalam are mone (son) and mole (daughter). Parents, uncles, aunties and even shopkeepers use them warmly for any younger person.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
ഇത് എന്റെ ചേട്ടനാIthu ente chettanaaThis is my elder brother
അമ്മ അടുക്കളയിലാAmma adukkalayilaaAmma’s in the kitchen
അച്ഛൻ വന്നുAchan vannuDad’s here (lit. father came)
ചേച്ചി സുഖമാണോ?Chechi sukhamaano?Chechi, how are you?

Grandparents

Words for grandparents vary by region and community, so use whatever your family uses. These are the most widely understood.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
അമ്മമ്മAmmammaGrandmother (mother’s mother)
അച്ഛമ്മAchammaGrandmother (father’s mother)
അമ്മൂമ്മAmmoommaGrandmother (common in many regions)
അപ്പൂപ്പൻAppooppanGrandfather
മുത്തശ്ശൻ / മുത്തശ്ശിMuthassan / MuthassiGrandfather / grandmother (general)

The respect rule: -etta and -echi

Malayalis rarely call elders by bare names. Add -chetta / -etta for older men and -chechi / -echi for older women: Rajesh-etta, Deepa-chechi. The same words are used for neighbours, colleagues and the man at the tea shop, as a sign of respect and warmth. Many wives traditionally call their husbands chetta. Using this rule correctly is the fastest way to sound naturally respectful.

Friend, buddy and best friend

Friend words switch by gender: koottukaaran for a male friend, koottukaari for a female friend. Among friends, Malayalis use playful slang that you’ll hear in every Malayalam film.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
കൂട്ടുകാരൻKoottukaaranFriend (male)
കൂട്ടുകാരിKoottukaariFriend (female)
സുഹൃത്ത്SuhruthuFriend (neutral, a little formal)
ചങ്ങാതിChangaathiPal, buddy
ഉറ്റ ചങ്ങാതിUtta changaathiBest friend
ചങ്ക്ChunkBestie (slang, lit. “heart”)
മച്ചാനേ!Machaane!Buddy! / Dude! (to a male friend)
അളിയാ!Aliyaa!Bro! (lit. brother-in-law; friendly slang)
എന്താ ഡാ? / എന്താ ഡീ?Entha da? / Entha di?What’s up? (to a male / female friend; very casual)
ഇത് എന്റെ കൂട്ടുകാരനാIthu ente koottukaaranaaThis is my friend

Da and di are strictly for close friends and people younger than you. Never use them with elders; use chetta or chechi instead.

I’m married / are you married?

Expect this question early when meeting a Malayali family. The formal word vivaahithan changes with gender, while the everyday version simply says “my wedding is done.”

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
ഞാൻ വിവാഹിതനാണ്Njaan vivaahithanaanuI’m married (man)
ഞാൻ വിവാഹിതയാണ്Njaan vivaahithayaanuI’m married (woman)
എന്റെ കല്യാണം കഴിഞ്ഞുEnte kalyaanam kazhinjuI’m married (everyday)
ഞാൻ അവിവാഹിതനാണ് / അവിവാഹിതയാണ്Njaan avivaahithanaanu / avivaahithayaanuI’m single (man / woman)
കല്യാണം കഴിഞ്ഞോ?Kalyaanam kazhinjo?Are you married?
എനിക്ക് രണ്ട് മക്കളുണ്ട്Enikku randu makkalunduI have two children
ഇത് എന്റെ ഭാര്യയാ / ഭർത്താവാIthu ente bhaaryayaa / bharthaavaaThis is my wife / husband

Put it together: a family video call

Ammamma: Mole, sukhamaano? Choru kazhicho? — Dear, are you well? Have you eaten?

You: Sukhamaanu, ammamma. Kazhichu! Ammamma kazhicho? — I’m well, Ammamma. I ate! Did you?

Ammamma: Kazhichu. Achanum ammayum evide? — I did. Where are your dad and mum?

You: Amma adukkalayilaa. Achan ippo varum. — Amma’s in the kitchen. Dad will come now.

Ammamma: Ente ponnu mole! — My precious girl!

Frequently asked questions

What do Amma and Achan mean?

Amma (അമ്മ) means mother and achan (അച്ഛൻ) means father. “Achanum ammayum” means father and mother.

What does Chetta mean in Malayalam?

Chetta (from chettan) means elder brother. It is also used respectfully for any older man, and traditionally by wives for husbands. The female form is chechi.

What do Malayalis call their grandparents?

Ammamma (mother’s mother), achamma (father’s mother), ammoomma, and appooppan or muthassan for grandfather. Usage varies by region.

How do you say friend in Malayalam?

Koottukaaran (male friend) or koottukaari (female friend). Suhruthu is a neutral word; utta changaathi means best friend, and machaane is the slang “buddy”.

How do you say I am married in Malayalam?

A man says “njaan vivaahithanaanu” and a woman says “njaan vivaahithayaanu”. In everyday speech, “ente kalyaanam kazhinju”.

What do mone and mole mean?

Mone means son and mole means daughter, used affectionately for any younger person.
