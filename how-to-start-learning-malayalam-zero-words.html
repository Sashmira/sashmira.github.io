Start with 20 words, not 2,000

If you know no Malayalam at all, the fastest start is a small set of words that combine into hundreds of sentences. These 20 cover most of what a beginner needs in the first week at home, on the phone or on a trip to Kerala. Learn them by saying them out loud, not by writing them.

Turn the 20 words into sentences

Put the words into these five frames and you can already hold a short exchange.

MalayalamRomanisedMeaning
എനിക്ക് ചായ വേണംenikku chaaya venamI want tea
വീട് എവിടെയാണ്?veedu evideyaanu?Where is the house?
ഇതിന് എത്രയാണ്?ithinu ethrayaanu?How much is this?
വെള്ളം ഉണ്ടോ?vellam undo?Is there water?
എനിക്ക് കുറച്ച് മലയാളം അറിയാംenikku kurachu malayaalam ariyaamI know a little Malayalam

The word enikku means "to me". Malayalam says "to me, tea is wanted" rather than "I want tea". You will meet this pattern again and again; it is explained fully in Malayalam sentence patterns for beginners.

Your first seven days

Day 1: Learn the greeting exchange sukhamaano? / sukham, nanni and say it to yourself ten times.
Day 2: Learn njaan, ningal, ithu, athu. Point at things and say ithu or athu.
Day 3: Learn venam / venda and the food words. Practise enikku chaaya venam and enikku venda.
Day 4: Learn enthu, evide, ethra. Ask three questions about things in your room.
Day 5: Learn undu / illa. Ask ___ undo? and answer yourself.
Day 6: Listen to the words with native audio in the listening library and copy the rhythm.
Day 7: Use five sentences with a real person, even if it is just a greeting and a thank you on a family call.

Three things not to do in week one

Don't start with the script

The Malayalam script is beautiful and worth learning, but it can wait a few weeks. Speak first with romanisation, then learn the letters with the alphabet guide.

Don't learn formal Malayalam

Textbook forms like enthaanu are correct, but at home people say enthaa. Learn what you will actually hear.

Don't wait to speak

Malayalis are delighted when a learner tries. A wrong word with a smile gets you further than silence.
