Why patterns work better than word lists

When I start a new learner, I don't hand them a vocabulary list. I hand them five or six sentence frames and let them swap one word at a time. A word list helps you recognise Malayalam. A frame lets you answer in Malayalam, and that is what a beginner needs on the first family call or the first visit to Kerala.

Two features of Malayalam make this approach unusually effective:

1. Many everyday feelings start with എനിക്ക് (enikku, "to me"). Where English says "I want", "I like", "I know" or "I can", Malayalam says "to me, it is wanted / liked / known / possible". Learn enikku once and four of the most useful sentence types open up.
2. Verbs don't change for the person. ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു (njaan pokunnu, I go), അവൻ പോകുന്നു (avan pokunnu, he goes), നമ്മൾ പോകുന്നു (nammal pokunnu, we go). Unlike Hindi or French, there is no ending to match with "I", "you" or "they". Once you know the verb, it works for everyone.

The "enikku" family: four patterns from one word

MalayalamRomanisedMeaning
എനിക്ക് വെള്ളം വേണംenikku vellam venamI want water
എനിക്ക് വേണ്ടenikku vendaI don't want it (polite refusal at the table)
എനിക്ക് കേരള ഭക്ഷണം ഇഷ്ടമാണ്enikku kerala bhakshanam ishtamaanuI like Kerala food
എനിക്ക് കുറച്ച് മലയാളം അറിയാംenikku kurachu malayaalam ariyaamI know a little Malayalam
എനിക്ക് അറിയില്ലenikku ariyillaI don't know
എനിക്ക് നാളെ വരാൻ പറ്റുംenikku naale varaan pattumI can come tomorrow
എനിക്ക് പറ്റില്ലenikku pattillaI can't (manage it)

Notice the shape: enikku + thing + feeling word. To make the negative, change the last word: venam → venda, ariyaam → ariyilla, pattum → pattilla, ishtamaanu → ishtamalla.

Six more frames you can use today

Malayalam frameRomanisedUse it for
___ എവിടെയാണ്?___ evideyaanu?Where is ___?  e.g. bus stop evideyaanu?  toilet evideyaanu?
___ ഉണ്ടോ?___ undo?Is there / do you have ___?  e.g. chaaya undo? (Is there tea?). Answer: undu (yes, there is) / illa (no)
ഇത് ___ ആണ്ithu ___ aanuThis is ___.  e.g. ithu ente ammayaanu (This is my mother)
ഇതിന് എത്രയാണ്?ithinu ethrayaanu?How much is this? Point and ask; works in any shop or market
ഒന്നുകൂടി പറയാമോ?onnu koodi parayaamo?Could you say that once more? Swap in pathukke parayaamo? (Could you say it slowly?)
ഞാൻ ___ പോവുകയാണ്njaan ___ povukayaanuI'm going to ___.  e.g. njaan veettilekku povukayaanu (I'm going home)

The ending -aamo? turns a verb into a polite request: tharaamo? (could you give?), sahaayikkaamo? (could you help?), parayaamo? (could you say?). It is the single most useful politeness tool for a beginner.

The mistakes I correct most often

Translating "I want" word for word

Learners say njaan venam. Malayalam needs the "to me" form: enikku venam. The same goes for like, know and can.

Dropping the question ending

Yes/no questions end in -o: undu (there is) becomes undo? (is there?). Without it, your question sounds like a statement.

Using nee with elders

Nee (you) is for close friends and younger people. With elders and strangers use ningal, or avoid "you" entirely, which Malayalam speakers often do.

A five-minute drill

  1. Say enikku ___ venam with five things around you: vellam (water), chaaya (tea), choru (rice), pusthakam (book), sahaayam (help).
  2. Turn each one into a question with ___ undo?
  3. Answer yourself: undu or illa.
  4. Finish with onnu koodi parayaamo? three times, faster each time.
