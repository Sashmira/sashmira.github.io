Malayalam Food & Drink: Words and Everyday Phrases

By Reshmi R Nair, Malayalam Linguist · Updated October 2026

Food is at the heart of Kerala life, and food Malayalam is some of the most rewarding you can learn. This guide covers the everyday food and drink words, then the phrases you’ll use at every meal: I’m hungry, have you eaten, let’s eat, it’s delicious, water please, ordering tea, and saying you’re vegetarian. Everything is everyday spoken Malayalam, with the script, an easy romanised spelling and the English meaning.

I’m hungry / I’m thirsty

Feelings like hunger are expressed with enikku (to me): literally “to me, it hungers.” Casually, people drop enikku and just say vishakkunnu.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
എനിക്ക് വിശക്കുന്നുEnikku vishakkunnuI’m hungry
നല്ല വിശപ്പുണ്ട്Nalla vishappunduI’m really hungry
എനിക്ക് ദാഹിക്കുന്നുEnikku daahikkunnuI’m thirsty
എന്തെങ്കിലും കഴിക്കണംEnthenkilum kazhikkanamI need to eat something

Have you eaten? (Choru kazhicho?)

Choru kazhicho? literally means “did you eat rice?”, but it’s really one of the most affectionate things a Malayali can ask: a way of saying “are you okay? I care about you.” Grandparents ask it on every phone call.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
ചോറ് കഴിച്ചോ?Choru kazhicho?Have you eaten?
ഭക്ഷണം കഴിച്ചോ?Bhakshanam kazhicho?Have you had food? (a little more formal)
ഉവ്വ്, കഴിച്ചുUvvu, kazhichuYes, I’ve eaten
ഇല്ല, ഇപ്പോ കഴിക്കുംIlla, ippo kazhikkumNot yet, I’ll eat now

Let’s eat / please eat

Malayali hosts keep serving until you stop them, so mathi (enough) is as useful as kazhikkaam (let’s eat).

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
നമുക്ക് കഴിക്കാംNammukku kazhikkaamLet’s eat
വരൂ, കഴിക്കാംVaroo, kazhikkaamCome, let’s eat
കഴിച്ചോളൂKazhicholooPlease eat / help yourself
ഒന്നൂടെ കഴിച്ചോളൂOnnoode kazhicholooHave some more
മതി, മതി!Mathi, mathi!Enough, enough! (I’m full)
വയറ് നിറഞ്ഞുVayaru niranjuI’m full (lit. my stomach is full)

It’s delicious

Complimenting the food is the quickest way to a Malayali cook’s heart. Ruchi means taste; adipoli and kidilan are the everyday slang for “awesome.”

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
നല്ല രുചി!Nalla ruchi!Really tasty!
അടിപൊളി!Adipoli!Awesome!
കിടിലൻ!Kidilan!Brilliant! / Top-class!
നല്ല ടേസ്റ്റ് ആയിരുന്നുNalla taste aayirunnuIt was really tasty
ഈ കറി അടിപൊളി!Ee curry adipoli!This curry is awesome!

Water: asking for and offering it

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
വെള്ളം വേണംVellam venamI want water
കുറച്ച് വെള്ളം തരാമോ?Kurachu vellam tharaamo?Could I have some water?
തണുത്ത വെള്ളംThanutha vellamCold water
ചൂടുവെള്ളംChooduvellamHot water
വെള്ളം കുടിക്കൂVellam kudikkooDrink some water

In many Kerala homes and restaurants you’ll be served warm jeerakavellam (cumin water) or chukkuvellam (dry-ginger water) instead of plain water. It’s safe and traditional; enjoy it.

Ordering at a tea shop

Kerala’s tea shops (chaayakkada) serve tea with snacks like parippu vada (lentil fritters) and pazham pori (banana fritters). Call the server chetta (brother).

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
ചേട്ടാ, ഒരു ചായChetta, oru chaayaBrother, one tea
രണ്ട് ചായ, ഒരെണ്ണം കടുപ്പത്തിൽRandu chaaya, orennam kaduppathilTwo teas, one strong
കഴിക്കാൻ എന്തുണ്ട്?Kazhikkaan enthundu?What is there to eat?
ഒരു പരിപ്പ് വടയുംOru parippu vadayumAnd a parippu vada
എത്രയായി?Ethrayaayi?How much is it?

I’m vegetarian / not spicy, please

English “vegetarian” is understood everywhere. Many Kerala dishes contain fish, so it’s worth saying meen venda too.

MalayalamRomanisedEnglish
ഞാൻ വെജിറ്റേറിയൻ ആണ്Njaan vegetarian aanuI’m vegetarian
ഇറച്ചിയും മീനും വേണ്ടIrachiyum meenum vendaNo meat or fish
വെജിറ്റേറിയൻ എന്തുണ്ട്?Vegetarian enthundu?What vegetarian food is there?
എരിവ് കുറച്ച് മതിErivu kurachu mathiNot too spicy, please

Put it together: at the table

Host: Choru kazhicho? — Have you eaten?

You: Illa, nalla vishappundu! — Not yet, I’m really hungry!

Host: Varoo, kazhikkaam. Meen curry undu. — Come, let’s eat. There’s fish curry.

You: Aahaa, nalla ruchi! Adipoli! — Wow, really tasty! Awesome!

Host: Onnoode kazhicholoo. — Have some more.

You: Mathi, mathi! Vayaru niranju. Nandi! — Enough, enough! I’m full. Thank you!

Frequently asked questions

How do you say “I’m hungry” in Malayalam?

Say “enikku vishakkunnu” (എനിക്ക് വിശക്കുന്നു). “I’m thirsty” is “enikku daahikkunnu”.

What does “choru kazhicho” mean?

Literally “did you eat rice?”, it means “have you eaten?” and is an affectionate way of asking how someone is. Reply “uvvu, kazhichu” (yes, I’ve eaten).

How do you say “delicious” in Malayalam?

Say “nalla ruchi!” (really tasty). Slang favourites are “adipoli!” and “kidilan!”

What is “water” in Malayalam?

Water is “vellam” (വെള്ളം). To ask for some, say “kurachu vellam tharaamo?”

How do you say “I’m vegetarian” in Malayalam?

Say “njaan vegetarian aanu”, and add “irachiyum meenum venda” (no meat or fish).
