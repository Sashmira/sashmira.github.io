Kidu! The Malayalam slang nobody teaches you

Textbook Malayalam gets you understood. Slang gets you adopted. The day you look at a friend’s new phone and say Kidu! instead of “very nice”, something shifts — you stop being a foreigner who studied Malayalam and become a person who speaks it.

Home › Resources › Kidu! slang
One rule before the list. Slang is peer language. Every word here is safe with friends and people your own age, and risky with elders, officials and strangers. Remember the direction: slang flows sideways and downward, never upward.

The praise dial — mild to maximum

കൊള്ളാംkollaam nice, not bad, good

Mild — safe almost anywhere. It literally means “it will do”, and Malayalis use it the way the British use “not bad”. The one word here you can say to your mother-in-law.

നല്ലതാണ്nallathaanu it is good

Neutral — safe everywhere.

അടിപൊളിadipoli superb, fantastic

Warm — informal but widely loved. The elder statesman of cool, around long enough that even parents say it. Related: adichu polikkuka, to have a blast.

പൊളിpoli excellent

Hot — youth speech. Online you will see it spelled pwoli — same word, social-media costume.

കിടുkidu awesome, killer

Hot — youth speech.

കിടുവേ!kiduvee! awesooome!

Maximum — stretched for emphasis. Stretching the vowel raises the volume: kiduuu, kiduvee.

The false friend that gets learners laughed at

തല്ലിപ്പൊളി thallippoli

It looks like it belongs to the poli praise family. It does not. It means worthless, trashy: thallippoli padam = a terrible movie. Learners who guess “extra-poli must mean extra good” get laughed at — warmly, but laughed at. Now you won’t.

The trouble-and-teasing kit

MalayalamMeaningTypical use

തള്ള്
thallu		bragging, tall talkAvante thallu ketto? — did you hear his bragging?
ചളി
chali		lame joke, cringe humourchali adikkuka — to crack bad jokes
വട്ടാണോ?

Handle with care. Thallu and vattu can genuinely insult. Never call out someone’s thallu publicly.
