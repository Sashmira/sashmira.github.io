The 25 Malayalam sentences that keep a grandparent's video call alive

By Reshmi R Nair, Malayalam Linguist · Updated October 2026

The Sunday call to Kerala follows a script. Grandma asks the same few questions, the child looks at the parent, the parent translates, and the call is over in six minutes. These twenty-five sentences let the child answer for themselves, which is the whole point of the call.

Families use different words for grandparents: ammoomma or ammachi for grandmother, appooppan or appachan for grandfather. Use whatever your family says; the sentences work the same way.

3. ഇപ്പോ കഴിക്കുംippo kazhikkumI'll eat now

How to use this list

Pick three sentences for this Sunday and put them on a sticky note next to the screen. The grandparent's job is to ask slowly and wait. The parent's job is to say nothing. Those few seconds of silence are where the Malayalam comes from.

For a shy child, start with the questions the child asks (8 to 12). Asking puts the child in control; answering can feel like a test.

Frequently asked questions

Our grandparents speak a strong local dialect. Which Malayalam should the child learn?

Theirs. Every dialect is correct for its speakers. Listen to how the grandparents ask the usual questions and use their words.

The child freezes on video. What helps?

Let the child ask the questions first, keep each call short, and celebrate any Malayalam answer, even a single word.

How many sentences should a child learn at once?

Three a week is plenty. Use them on every call until they come out without thinking, then add three more.
