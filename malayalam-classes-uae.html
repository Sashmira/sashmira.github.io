Gulf Malayalis: fluent at home, stuck at school

The Gulf is different from the US, UK or Canada, and the pattern here is different too. In Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah, many Malayali children hear a great deal of Malayalam at home — and still cannot hold a proper conversation with grandparents in Kerala.

What usually happens is that the child’s Malayalam becomes a household dialect: enough for “where are my shoes” and “I am hungry,” but not enough for feelings, stories, or a long phone call with an ammachi who speaks no English. Meanwhile school runs entirely in English, and friendships run in English and Hindi.

Structured lessons close that specific gap — moving a child from functional household Malayalam to real conversational Malayalam they can actually use on a video call to Kerala.

Common questions from Gulf learners

My child speaks some Malayalam at home but cannot talk to grandparents. Why?

Because household Malayalam is a small, repetitive set of phrases. Real conversation needs a wider range — feelings, past events, opinions, storytelling. That is exactly what lessons build.

My child studies Malayalam as a second language at school. Is this different?

Very. School Malayalam is script-and-exam focused. These lessons are spoken-first and conversational. They complement school work rather than repeat it, and children often find they finally understand why the school material matters.

Do the lesson times work with a Dubai work schedule?

Yes, easily. The UAE is only 1.5 hours behind India, so evening lessons after work are straightforward.

I am not Malayali. Can I still learn?

Yes. A good number of my Gulf students have no Malayali background at all. Everything is taught in English from zero.
