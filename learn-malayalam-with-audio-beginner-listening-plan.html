Learn Malayalam with audio: beginner listening plan

Train your ear before grammar: a 15-minute daily routine, the five Malayalam sound contrasts English speakers miss, and a four-week plan with native audio.

Open pronunciation guide

Why listening comes first in Malayalam

Most of my learners can already read a little English-style romanisation, but they freeze when a Malayali speaks at normal speed. The problem is rarely vocabulary. It is that Malayalam runs words together, swallows endings in casual speech, and uses sounds English has no letters for. Your ear has to learn those before your mouth can copy them.

The good news: ear training needs only 10 to 15 minutes a day, and you can do it while cooking or commuting.

The sound contrasts to train first

These pairs change the meaning of a word. If you can hear the difference, you will understand far more, and be understood.

MalayalamRomanisedWhat to listen for
മല / മാലmala / maalaShort vs long vowel: mala is a hill, maala a necklace
പടി / പട്ടിpadi / pattiSingle vs double consonant: padi is a step, patti a dog. Hold the double sound slightly longer
പനി / പണിpani / paniTwo different n sounds: പനി (fever) has the tongue behind the teeth; പണി (work) curls the tongue back
മഴmazhaThe famous zh (ഴ): tongue curled back without touching, like a soft r-y sound. It means rain
വള / വലvala / valaTwo l sounds: വള (bangle) curls the tongue back; വല (net) is a light English-like l

Play each pair slowly, then at normal speed. Don't try to produce them perfectly yet; just notice that they are different.

The daily 15-minute routine

Minutes 1 to 3: listen without pausing. Play a short clip of everyday Malayalam once and only notice rhythm and where the pauses fall.
Minutes 4 to 7: catch the known words. Replay it and raise a finger every time you hear a word you know, such as enthu (what), evide (where), venam (want) or illa (no).
Minutes 8 to 11: shadow. Speak along with the audio a split second behind the speaker. Copy the melody first and the words second.
Minutes 12 to 15: record one line. Record yourself saying one sentence, compare it with the original, and note one sound to improve tomorrow.

A four-week listening plan

WeekListen toYou should be able to
Week 1Greetings and replies: sukhamaano? (are you well?), sukham (I'm well), nanni (thanks)Recognise a greeting and reply without thinking
Week 2Family words: amma, achan, chechi, chettan, ammoommaFollow who is being talked about on a family call
Week 3Questions: enthu, evide, eppol, aaru, ethra (what, where, when, who, how much)Tell a question from a statement and catch the question word
Week 4Short everyday dialogues: ordering tea, asking directions, a phone check-inGet the gist of a 20-second exchange on first listen
