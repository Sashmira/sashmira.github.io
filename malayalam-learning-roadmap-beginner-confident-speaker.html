Malayalam learning roadmap: beginner to confident speaker

Six stages from first greetings to confident everyday speaker, each with a can-do goal, a realistic timing and the actual Malayalam you should be able to say.

Open the main roadmap

How to use this roadmap

Each stage has a "can-do" goal and real sentences you should be able to say before moving on. The timings assume 15 to 20 minutes of practice a day. If a stage takes longer, that's fine; skipping ahead is what makes learners stall.

The six stages

Stage 1: First sounds and greetings (weeks 1 to 2)

You can: greet people and say thank you naturally.

  • സുഖമാണോ? (sukhamaano?): are you well?
  • സുഖം, നന്ദി (sukham, nanni): I'm well, thanks

Focus on: the long and short vowels and the zh sound (listening plan).

Stage 2: Introducing yourself (weeks 3 to 4)

You can: say who you are and where you are from.

  • എന്റെ പേര് ___ ആണ് (ente peru ___ aanu): my name is ___
  • ഞാൻ ലണ്ടനിൽ നിന്നാണ് (njaan londonil ninnaanu): I'm from London

Focus on: the -il ending for "in" and ninnu for "from".

Stage 3: Wants, likes and questions (month 2)

You can: ask for things and ask simple questions.

  • എനിക്ക് ചായ വേണം (enikku chaaya venam): I want tea
  • കടയിൽ പാൽ ഉണ്ടോ? (kadayil paal undo?): is there milk at the shop?

Focus on: the enikku pattern and question words (sentence patterns, question words).

Stage 4: Past and future (month 3)

You can: say what you did and what you will do.

  • ഞാൻ കഴിച്ചു (njaan kazhichu): I ate
  • ഞാൻ നാളെ വരും (njaan naale varum): I'll come tomorrow

Focus on: past endings (-chu, -thu, -i) and the future -um, which is the same for every person.

Stage 5: A family phone call (months 4 to 5)

You can: keep a five-minute call going with a relative.

  • എല്ലാവർക്കും സുഖമാണോ? (ellaavarkkum sukhamaano?): is everyone well?
  • ഒന്നുകൂടി പറയാമോ? (onnu koodi parayaamo?): could you say that again?

Focus on: listening at natural speed and the casual forms people really use (enthaa for enthaanu).

Stage 6: Confident everyday speaker (month 6 onwards)

You can: talk about your day, your plans and your feelings, and follow a group conversation.

  • ഇന്ന് ഒരുപാട് പണിയുണ്ടായിരുന്നു (innu orupaadu paniyundaayirunnu): I had a lot of work today

Focus on: linking sentences, reading the script, and regular correction from a teacher.

How to know you're ready to move on

You answer without translating

When sukhamaano? gets an instant sukham, the stage is done.

You can make new sentences

If you can swap words into a frame you've never said before, the pattern is yours.

Native speakers don't slow down for you

The real test of stages 5 and 6 is when relatives forget you're a learner.
